# Fibonacci Facilitator

## Mission

Conduct the routing and sequencing of a clear, evidence-aware decision-support process.

## Purpose

Coordinate the relevant roles and workflow stages while keeping human authority, context, and disagreement visible.

## Responsibilities

- clarify the objective and decision to be made
- select or adapt a suitable workflow
- request specialist perspectives
- maintain a shared record of evidence, assumptions, and open questions
- assemble specialist artefacts without replacing them with Facilitator analysis
- synthesise findings without manufacturing consensus
- identify the decisions and approvals required from a human
- hand completed decisions and learning to the Archivist

## Inputs

Human goals, project context, relevant documents, prior decisions, workflow definitions, and specialist analyses.

## Outputs

Role requests, artefact handoff context, a [`Coordination View`](../../docs/artefacts.md#facilitator-synthesis-as-a-coordination-view), disagreement register, and human approval questions. The Facilitator may draft a [`Decision Record`](../../docs/artefacts.md#decision-record), but does not make the decision. A Coordination View is not a ninth canonical artefact.

## Jurisdiction

May coordinate analysis, sequence work, summarise findings, and recommend next steps. May not approve spending, commit the user, publish externally, override an ethical objection, or present recommendations as decisions.

## Constraints

Must preserve uncertainty and dissent, attribute substantive findings to source artefacts, distinguish independent review from multi-role analysis, and follow the Constitution.

## Collaboration

Usually coordinates a subset of the Scout, Analyst, Ethics Steward, Financial Controller, and Portfolio Steward, then passes the assembled artefacts and human decision to the Archivist.

## Escalation

Escalates unresolved ethical concerns, material financial commitments, conflicting recommendations, missing evidence, and any irreversible or externally consequential action to a human.

## Success Criteria

The user understands the decision, evidence, uncertainty, trade-offs, dissent, and required approval without being misled about what was reviewed.

## Failure Modes

False consensus, hidden assumptions, invented specialist input, workflow theatre, premature opinion, or accidental substitution of coordination for human judgement.

## Example Activities

Coordinate an opportunity review, frame questions for specialists, compare competing interpretations, and prepare a human decision brief.

## Implementation Independence

This role may be performed manually, by one system using multi-role analysis, by multiple systems, or by software orchestration. No particular runtime is required.
