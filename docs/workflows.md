# Workflows

> *Workflows are repeatable processes that transform ideas into knowledge, products, and sustainable value. They capture proven ways of working so that both humans and AI agents can collaborate consistently.*

A workflow is not a rigid procedure.

It is a structured guide that can be adapted to the needs of a particular project while preserving its essential stages and decision points.

---

# Purpose

Workflows exist to:

- reduce unnecessary complexity
- encourage evidence-based decisions
- improve consistency
- capture organisational knowledge
- support collaboration
- enable automation
- make successful practices repeatable

Every completed workflow should leave the operating system stronger than before.

---

# Design Principles

Every workflow should be:

- **Goal-oriented** — designed to achieve a specific outcome.
- **Evidence-driven** — based on measurable information where practical.
- **Human-centred** — requiring human judgement at key decisions.
- **Composable** — capable of being combined with other workflows.
- **Continuously improving** — refined through practical experience.

---

# The Standard Lifecycle

Most work within Fibonacci OS follows a common lifecycle.

```text
Observe
    ↓
Discover
    ↓
Research
    ↓
Validate
    ↓
Plan
    ↓
Build
    ↓
Launch
    ↓
Operate
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Improve
    ↓
Retire or Continue
```

Projects may skip or repeat stages depending on their goals.

---

# Stage 1 — Observe

Observe the world before attempting to change it.

Activities may include:

- identifying problems
- recognising unmet needs
- noticing trends
- recording ideas
- collecting observations

Primary outputs:

- notes
- ideas
- opportunities

Typical AI support:

- Opportunity Scout
- Archivist

---

# Stage 2 — Discover

Transform observations into candidate opportunities.

Questions include:

- Is this a real problem?
- Who experiences it?
- How frequently?
- Why has it not already been solved?

Outputs:

- opportunity briefs
- research questions
- initial hypotheses

---

# Stage 3 — Research

Gather evidence before committing resources.

Research may include:

- market analysis
- technical feasibility
- competitor analysis
- customer interviews
- literature review
- financial investigation

Outputs:

- evidence
- assumptions
- risks
- unanswered questions

Typical AI support:

- Opportunity Scout
- Skeptical Analyst

---

# Stage 4 — Validate

Test assumptions using the smallest practical experiment.

Validation may involve:

- interviews
- surveys
- prototypes
- landing pages
- pilot programmes
- usability testing

The objective is learning, not perfection.

Outputs:

- validated assumptions
- invalidated assumptions
- new questions

---

# Stage 5 — Plan

If validation supports continued investment, create a plan.

Planning includes:

- objectives
- scope
- priorities
- milestones
- budgets
- risks
- success metrics

Outputs:

- project plan
- roadmap
- decision records

Typical AI support:

- Financial Controller
- Portfolio Steward

---

# Stage 6 — Build

Create the product or service.

Examples include:

- software
- documentation
- educational resources
- physical products
- services
- automation

Build iteratively.

Deliver working results early and improve continuously.

---

# Stage 7 — Launch

Release the project to real users.

Activities include:

- publishing
- deployment
- marketing
- onboarding
- customer support

The launch is the beginning of learning, not the end of development.

---

# Stage 8 — Operate

Operate sustainably.

Activities include:

- customer support
- maintenance
- improvements
- monitoring
- financial management
- documentation

Healthy operations are more valuable than rapid expansion.

---

# Stage 9 — Review

Pause regularly to reflect.

Questions include:

- What worked?
- What failed?
- What surprised us?
- What evidence changed our understanding?
- What should we improve?

Outputs:

- review reports
- lessons learned
- updated workflows

Every project should include scheduled reviews.

---

# Stage 10 — Improve

Apply lessons from previous work.

Improvements may affect:

- products
- documentation
- templates
- workflows
- AI agents
- organisational practices

Knowledge should accumulate over time.

---

# Stage 11 — Retire or Continue

Not every project should continue indefinitely.

Possible outcomes include:

- continue unchanged
- continue with improvements
- pause
- merge with another project
- archive
- retire permanently

Ending a project responsibly is considered a successful outcome when it frees resources for more valuable work.

---

# Human Decision Points

Certain decisions always require human judgement.

Examples include:

- approving significant investment
- publishing public content
- handling legal or ethical issues
- accepting financial risk
- retiring major projects
- changing strategic direction

AI agents provide recommendations.

Humans remain accountable.

---

# AI Collaboration

Different agents contribute throughout the workflow.

| Stage | Primary Agents |
|--------|----------------|
| Observe | Opportunity Scout, Archivist |
| Discover | Opportunity Scout |
| Research | Opportunity Scout, Skeptical Analyst |
| Validate | Skeptical Analyst, Financial Controller |
| Plan | Financial Controller, Portfolio Steward |
| Build | Domain-specific agents |
| Launch | Marketing and Product agents |
| Operate | Portfolio Steward, Financial Controller |
| Review | Archivist, Skeptical Analyst |
| Improve | All relevant agents |

Future versions of Fibonacci OS may introduce additional specialised agents.

---

# Workflow Composition

Simple workflows can be combined to create larger systems.

Examples include:

- Customer Interview Workflow
- Market Research Workflow
- Product Launch Workflow
- Quarterly Review Workflow
- Open Source Release Workflow

Projects should reuse existing workflows whenever practical rather than inventing new ones.

---

# Continuous Evolution

No workflow is considered complete.

Every execution provides opportunities to:

- simplify steps
- improve documentation
- remove unnecessary work
- automate repetitive tasks
- increase clarity
- improve outcomes

Operational excellence emerges through continual refinement rather than perfect initial design.

---

# Success

A workflow is successful when it consistently helps people make better decisions, reduce unnecessary effort, and create meaningful value.

The best workflow is not the most detailed.

It is the one that reliably guides humans and AI agents from uncertainty to understanding, from ideas to evidence, and from intention to lasting impact.
