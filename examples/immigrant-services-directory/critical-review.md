# Critical Review: Structured Finland Immigrant Services Directory

**Type:** Critical Review  
**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Author or role:** Skeptical Analyst  
**Related project or opportunity:** Finland Immigrant Services Directory  
**Review date:** After external discovery research

## Proposal Under Review

Build an authoritative, structured directory of Finnish public websites, digital
portals, official services, regional resources, and trusted organisations for
immigrants, foreign workers, international students, refugees, and relocating
families.

## Evidence Examined

- The Opportunity Brief in this directory.
- The opportunity statement supplied by the human sponsor.
- The Fibonacci OS principles and artefact contract.

No external user, market, service, maintenance, or competitor research was
supplied or conducted. No conclusion about current Finnish information quality
should be treated as established.

## Assumptions and Dependencies

- The problem is sufficiently painful or consequential to justify a new
  intervention.
- Users would prefer a curated directory to search engines, existing official
  navigation, or trusted human intermediaries.
- “Authoritative” sources can be identified and their status checked over time.
- A directory can remain current despite organisational, regional, language, and
  policy changes.
- The audience can be segmented into useful tasks without creating exclusion or
  harmful simplification.
- The team can obtain consent and recruit representative participants for
  discovery testing.

## Counterarguments or Competing Explanations

1. The main problem may be language, literacy, digital access, or legal
   complexity rather than link discovery.
2. Existing official services may already provide adequate information, while
   the proposed directory would add duplication.
3. The highest-value intervention may be a small set of task-specific guides,
   translation support, or referrals rather than a catalogue.
4. People may trust local helpers more than a new central directory, even if the
   directory is well structured.
5. The maintenance burden may outweigh the convenience created for users.

## Material Risks and Failure Conditions

- A wrong or stale entry causes a user to miss a deadline, service, benefit, or
  protection pathway.
- The directory implies eligibility or legal advice that it cannot safely
  provide.
- Coverage favours well-resourced organisations and excludes informal,
  minority-language, rural, or disability-relevant support.
- “Trusted” becomes an opaque editorial judgement without published criteria.
- A broad audience makes the taxonomy too complex to navigate.
- The project measures number of links rather than successful user outcomes and
  maintenance quality.

## Findings

### Strengths

- The opportunity is concrete enough to generate observable discovery tasks.
- A structured, attributed, reviewable format could support evidence and
  stewardship better than an unmaintained link list.
- The idea is compatible with open, local-first, reusable knowledge if privacy
  and maintenance are handled carefully.
- A small prototype could test learning without committing to a national
  product.

### Weaknesses

- There is currently no evidence of unmet demand or superiority over existing
  alternatives.
- The word “authoritative” creates a high burden of provenance, review, and
  scope control.
- The audience and service landscape are too broad for an initial build.
- Sustainability and editorial ownership are unresolved.

## Evidence Needed Next

- landscape review of existing official and community resources
- interviews or usability sessions across a deliberately narrow set of user
  contexts
- source-authority and update-frequency tests
- accessibility and language review
- maintenance-time estimate based on a small sample of entries
- safety review of how directory content could be mistaken for personal advice

## Recommendation

Do not proceed directly to a national directory. Proceed only, if the human
approves, with a small discovery experiment focused on a few tasks and a small
number of attributed entries. The experiment should be designed to disprove the
need for a new directory as readily as to support it.

## Confidence

Medium that the opportunity contains significant unknowns and should be
narrowed; low that a new directory is the best intervention.

## Requested Decision or Test

Human decision: approve a bounded discovery experiment, revise its audience and
task scope, or stop pending external evidence.
