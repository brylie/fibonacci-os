# Ethics Assessment: Structured Finland Immigrant Services Directory

**Type:** Ethics Assessment  
**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Author or role:** Ethics Steward  
**Related project or opportunity:** Finland Immigrant Services Directory  
**Review date:** Before any participant recruitment or public release

## Proposal and Scope

Test whether a small, structured directory of attributed Finnish services and support resources can help people complete selected discovery tasks. This assessment covers the proposed discovery experiment and the future product direction; it does not approve either one.

## Affected Parties

- immigrants, foreign workers, international students, refugees, and relocating families
- people with limited Finnish, Swedish, or English proficiency
- people with disabilities or limited digital access
- people facing urgent, precarious, or protection-related circumstances
- children and family members affected by a household's access to services
- public agencies, municipalities, community organisations, and volunteers whose information is represented
- researchers or maintainers responsible for reviewing entries

These groups are proposed for consideration, not evidence that they have been consulted.

## Potential Benefits and Harms

Potential benefits include reduced confusion, improved discoverability, clearer source attribution, and reusable public knowledge.

Potential harms include:

- directing someone to outdated, unsuitable, or inaccessible support
- creating false confidence through the label “authoritative”
- exposing people to surveillance, scams, discrimination, or unsafe contact routes
- excluding less visible organisations or treating state sources as sufficient for every need
- increasing burden on organisations whose information is copied without consent or context
- causing users to treat general information as personalised legal, immigration, health, or financial advice

The harms may be higher for users with fewer alternatives or more urgent needs.

## Privacy, Consent, and Autonomy

The initial experiment should not require participants to disclose immigration status, case details, identity documents, benefit information, or other sensitive personal information. Participants should be able to test generic tasks, withdraw without penalty, and decline recording.

The prototype should avoid user accounts, behavioural tracking, and unnecessary analytics. Any later data collection requires a separate privacy review and human approval.

## Fairness, Accessibility, and Power

The directory must not define “trusted” solely through institutional status, popularity, language visibility, or the maintainer's assumptions. Criteria should be explicit and reviewable.

The experiment should consider language, readability, screen-reader access, mobile access, low bandwidth, regional variation, and users who rely on human help. It should include a way to report an inaccurate, inaccessible, or harmful entry.

The project should not imply that one taxonomy or user journey represents all newcomers.

## Environmental Considerations

The initial Markdown prototype has minimal likely technical impact. Future hosting, crawling, search, and monitoring choices should be proportionate to the value created and should avoid unnecessary data collection or compute.

## Evidence and Uncertainty

No external research or participant input is available in this walkthrough. The risk analysis is based on the opportunity statement, the proposed operating model, and foreseeable failure modes. Severity and likelihood require further investigation.

## Mitigations and Safeguards

- narrow the experiment to low-risk discovery tasks
- attribute every entry and record the date checked
- separate directory information from advice or eligibility decisions
- define inclusion, exclusion, and review criteria before collecting entries
- provide a correction or challenge route
- avoid sensitive participant data and unnecessary tracking
- test accessibility and language assumptions explicitly
- include a visible limitation statement and escalation path for urgent needs
- require human review before publication

## Residual Risk

Residual risk remains material because even accurately transcribed information can become stale, context-dependent, or unsafe when users apply it to personal circumstances. A small experiment can test discoverability and misunderstanding, but it cannot establish that a directory is authoritative for all users or situations.

## Recommendation or Escalation

Proceed only with a bounded, non-public or carefully controlled experiment after human approval of scope, participant safeguards, source criteria, and review responsibilities. Do not use “authoritative” as a public product claim until provenance, maintenance, and user-safety evidence supports it.

## Confidence

Medium that the ethical risks are material and must shape the experiment; low regarding their observed frequency because no affected people have yet been consulted.

## Human Approval Required

Explicit approval is required before participant recruitment, collection of any personal data, public release, representation of an organisation, or use of the word “authoritative” in public-facing material.
