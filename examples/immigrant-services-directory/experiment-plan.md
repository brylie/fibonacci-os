# Draft Experiment Plan: Narrow Discovery of Finnish Service Information

**Type:** Experiment Plan  
**Status:** Draft — requires human approval  
**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Author or role:** Opportunity Scout, assembled by Fibonacci Facilitator  
**Related project or opportunity:** Finland Immigrant Services Directory  
**Review date:** Before recruitment or external publication

## Question

Can a small, clearly attributed, task-focused Markdown directory help a small
group of prospective users or helpers find selected Finnish relocation
information with less confusion or uncertainty than their current approach?

## Hypothesis

For a narrow set of common relocation tasks, a structured page that labels
source, audience, region, language, date checked, and limitations will improve
task completion or confidence without creating unacceptable misunderstanding or
maintenance burden.

This hypothesis does not claim that the directory is authoritative or that it
will improve outcomes for all groups.

## Intervention or Method

1. Select two or three low-risk discovery tasks with human approval.
2. Identify a small candidate set of public sources through external research.
3. Record each source's URL, owner or attribution, intended audience, region,
   language, date checked, and known limitations.
4. Create a simple Markdown prototype containing only the selected entries.
5. Ask a small, deliberately recruited group of prospective users or helpers to
   complete the tasks using the current approach and the prototype, in an order
   that reduces learning bias where practical.
6. Conduct a short debrief focused on what they found, misunderstood, trusted,
   or could not use.
7. Record the maintenance time and any corrections required during the
   experiment.

The exact task list, participant plan, source list, and comparison method
require human approval.

## Participants or Data

Suggested starting point: a small group of approximately five to eight
participants or helpers representing more than one relevant context. This is a
proposed design parameter, not a researched requirement.

Do not collect immigration status, case details, identity documents, benefit
records, contact lists, or other sensitive personal data. Generic scenarios may
be used. Participants must give informed consent, be able to withdraw, and not
be pressured to disclose personal circumstances.

## Measures and Observations

- whether the participant reaches the intended source or task endpoint
- time or effort in broad, non-precision terms
- wrong turns, uncertainty, and requests for clarification
- whether attribution and limitations are understood
- accessibility, language, device, or regional barriers encountered
- perceived usefulness and trust, without treating confidence as proof of
  accuracy
- time required to verify and format each entry
- corrections, stale links, or contradictory information discovered

## Learning Criteria

The hypothesis would be weakened if:

- participants do not experience a meaningful discovery problem
- the prototype adds little value over current methods
- the structure increases confusion or false confidence
- the source-review burden is clearly disproportionate to the value
- the selected tasks are not representative enough to justify continuation

The hypothesis would be strengthened, but not proven, if participants complete
selected tasks more reliably, understand source limits, and the maintenance
burden appears manageable for the narrow scope.

## Resources and Timebox

Use a small, human-approved time and cash cap. The specific cap is intentionally
unresolved because the responsible human has not yet provided a budget or
capacity record.

The experiment should be short enough to stop before a national taxonomy, full
website, automated crawler, translation system, or recurring service commitment
is created.

## Ethical Safeguards

- no unnecessary personal data or tracking
- clear distinction between directory information and personal advice
- no public “authoritative” claim during the experiment
- source attribution and date-checked fields for every entry
- accessibility and language limitations recorded
- correction and safety concerns escalated to a human
- human review before any public release

## Stop or Escalation Conditions

Stop or escalate if:

- a source appears unsafe, misleading, inaccessible, or materially stale
- participants misunderstand the prototype as official or personalised advice
- sensitive information is collected or exposed
- the work requires legal, immigration, health, or financial advice
- the review burden exceeds the approved cap
- the experiment reveals that another intervention is more appropriate

## Owner

Not assigned. A human must name the experiment owner and the person responsible
for source review before approval.

## Requested Approval

Human approval is requested for:

- whether to run the experiment at all
- task and audience scope
- participant safeguards and any compensation
- time and cash cap
- source inclusion criteria
- review ownership
- publication and data-handling boundaries
