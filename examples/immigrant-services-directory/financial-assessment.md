# Financial Assessment: Structured Finland Immigrant Services Directory

**Type:** Financial Assessment  
**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Author or role:** Financial Controller  
**Related project or opportunity:** Finland Immigrant Services Directory  
**Review date:** After the discovery experiment has measured maintenance effort and user value

## Scope, Period, and Currency

This assessment covers a proposed discovery experiment and the sustainability questions for a possible directory. No numeric budget has been approved. Currency would be EUR for a Finland-based operation; the period is the experiment period and an eventual ongoing-maintenance period, both currently unspecified.

## Inputs and Assumptions

Available inputs are the opportunity brief, specialist assessments, and the stated architectural constraints. The following are assumptions requiring measurement:

- a small Markdown prototype can be created with limited cost
- source discovery, verification, accessibility review, translation or language support, and maintenance will consume human time
- ongoing value depends more on accuracy and usefulness than on the number of entries
- a national scope would require substantially more stewardship than a narrow task-based scope
- funding, ownership, and maintenance responsibility are not yet defined

No external cost data or willingness-to-pay evidence is available.

## Costs and Resource Needs

Likely cost categories are:

- opportunity discovery and user research
- source verification and structured data entry
- editorial review and update monitoring
- accessibility and language testing
- participant recruitment or compensation, if used
- hosting, domain, search, backups, and security if a public service is later built
- governance, correction handling, and incident response

The largest early uncertainty is likely maintenance and review time rather than initial file creation. This must be measured, not assumed.

## Revenue, Pricing, or Savings Logic

No revenue model is recommended at this stage. Possible forms of value include time saved by users and helpers, reduced confusion, better access to existing services, reusable public knowledge, and learning about information stewardship.

Potential funding models—grant, institutional sponsorship, donation, volunteer contribution, or another arrangement—would carry different independence and sustainability risks. They should not be selected before the scope and maintenance burden are understood.

## Cash Implications

The proposed experiment can be designed with a small and explicitly capped cash exposure, but the cap has not been set. Human approval is needed for participant compensation, paid research, hosting commitments, or external services.

The future project could create recurring obligations even if the first prototype is inexpensive. A free initial build must not be treated as evidence of sustainable operation.

## Scenarios

### Downside: stop after discovery

The experiment finds little unmet need, high misunderstanding, or disproportionate maintenance cost. The financial exposure is limited to the approved experiment, and the learning is preserved.

### Base: narrow maintained resource

A small task-based resource demonstrates useful outcomes and manageable review effort. Continued work would still require a named owner, funding or protected capacity, and a review cadence.

### Upside: broader public utility

The resource shows strong value across contexts and attracts sustainable stewardship. This scenario is speculative and would introduce higher editorial, governance, accessibility, and support obligations.

## Sensitivity and Material Financial Risks

The decision is most sensitive to:

- time required to verify and update entries
- number of tasks and regions included
- participant recruitment and accessibility costs
- expected review frequency as services change
- whether external funding creates dependence or editorial pressure
- whether users receive enough value to justify ongoing maintenance

No quantitative sensitivity analysis is possible until the experiment measures these drivers.

## Non-Financial Value Not Represented by the Model

Potential non-financial value includes improved access to public information, reduced stress, stronger community support, reusable open knowledge, and learning about responsible information curation. Potential negative value includes harm from stale information, exclusion, or false authority. These outcomes must remain visible rather than being collapsed into revenue.

## Recommendation

Approve financial planning only for a small, capped experiment with explicit time and cash limits. Do not approve a full directory, revenue model, or recurring operating commitment until the experiment measures user value, maintenance effort, and ethical safeguards.

## Confidence

Medium that the key financial issue is ongoing stewardship rather than initial construction; low on all numeric estimates because none have been researched.

## Human Approval Required

Approval is required for the experiment budget and cap, participant costs, paid tools or hosting, any funding relationship, and any transition to recurring maintenance.
