# Opportunity Brief: Structured Finland Immigrant Services Directory

**Type:** Opportunity Brief  
**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Author or role:** Opportunity Scout  
**Related project or opportunity:** Finland Immigrant Services Directory  
**Review date:** After initial external discovery research

## Question or Observation

Could a structured, maintained directory make it easier for people arriving in
or moving within Finland to find trustworthy public services, digital portals,
regional resources, and organisations relevant to their situation?

The proposed audience spans immigrants, foreign workers, international students,
refugees, and relocating families. These groups may have different needs,
rights, languages, regions, and urgency levels. The opportunity statement does
not establish that one directory would serve them equally well.

## Affected People or Context

Potentially affected groups include:

- people preparing to move to Finland
- people who have recently arrived
- foreign workers and international students
- refugees and people with protection-related needs
- family members relocating together or joining someone already in Finland
- helpers, advisors, educators, employers, and community organisations

These are opportunity hypotheses, not researched user segments. The first
investigation should identify distinct tasks and contexts rather than assume one
universal user journey.

## Current Alternatives

The opportunity statement implies that relevant information is distributed
across public websites, digital portals, official services, regional resources,
and trusted organisations. This distribution, its usability, and the quality of
existing directories have not been independently verified in this walkthrough.

External validation is required to identify:

- existing national and regional directories
- authoritative source owners
- duplication and gaps
- language and accessibility support
- how people currently search and decide which source to trust

## Evidence

### Direct evidence

- The human sponsor supplied the opportunity statement.
- Fibonacci OS prioritises evidence, ethical value, local ownership, reusable
  knowledge, and small experiments.
- The proposed product would depend on information that can change and therefore
  would require ongoing stewardship.

### Not yet established

- the frequency or severity of the discovery problem
- whether people fail to find services using existing search methods
- which audiences and tasks matter most
- whether a new directory would be more useful than improving or linking
  existing resources
- whether the information can be maintained accurately and sustainably

## Assumptions

1. People need a more structured way to discover relevant services.
2. Existing sources are difficult to compare or navigate for at least some
   users.
3. A curated directory could improve trust or task completion.
4. Authoritative sources can be identified and monitored.
5. The project can define a safe boundary between directory information and
   regulated or individual advice.
6. A small, maintained scope can create learning before a national directory is
   attempted.

All six assumptions require testing.

## Risks and Exclusions

- stale, incomplete, or incorrectly classified information could cause harm
- users may mistake the directory for official advice or a guarantee of
  eligibility
- refugees and other vulnerable users may face higher consequences from
  incorrect guidance
- centralising links could increase privacy, security, or surveillance concerns
  if user data is collected
- a national scope could create an unmaintainable editorial burden
- “trusted organisation” may be subjective or politically contested
- language, disability, literacy, device, and regional access needs may be
  overlooked

## Opportunity Hypothesis

If a narrowly scoped directory presents clearly attributed, structured,
accessible, and regularly reviewable links for a small set of high-priority
relocation tasks, then users and helpers may complete those discovery tasks with
less confusion and greater confidence than they experience today.

This is a testable hypothesis, not a claim that the opportunity is proven.

## Proposed Investigation

Conduct a small discovery and usability experiment before building a national
catalogue:

1. Select two or three concrete relocation tasks, such as finding official
   starting information, understanding a regional service path, or locating
   support for a specific audience.
2. Identify a small candidate set of sources through external research and
   record source authority, date checked, audience, region, language, and access
   conditions.
3. Ask a small, ethically recruited group of prospective users or helpers to
   find the relevant information using current search methods and a simple
   Markdown prototype.
4. Observe task completion, uncertainty, misinterpretation, missing information,
   and maintenance effort.

The sample, tasks, sources, and measures require human review before execution.

## Confidence

Low. Confidence is high only that the opportunity is sufficiently concrete to
investigate; confidence is low regarding user demand, unmet need, feasibility,
and sustainability.

## Requested Next Action

Human decision: approve, revise, or reject a tightly scoped discovery
experiment. Do not approve national product development on the basis of this
brief.
