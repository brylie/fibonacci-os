**Status:** Draft  
**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Author or role:** Portfolio Steward

**Review date:** After the discovery experiment or at the next portfolio review

## Portfolio Question

Does a narrowly scoped investigation of a Finland immigrant-services directory fit the portfolio's purpose and current capacity, and what should be deferred or protected if it proceeds?

## Relevant Projects, Assets, and Objectives

Known relevant assets in this walkthrough are the Fibonacci OS documentation, role model, artefact model, and the stated interest in ethical, evidence-based, reusable public value. No active venture list, capacity record, competing project register, or existing directory inventory was supplied.

The project could align with:

- open knowledge and local ownership
- evidence-based opportunity discovery
- reusable documentation and information stewardship
- ethical value creation for people navigating complex systems

These are strategic-fit hypotheses, not a complete portfolio assessment.

## Capacity and Dependencies

The likely dependencies include user research, source discovery, editorial verification, accessibility and language review, privacy and safety review, and ongoing maintenance. A person or team responsible for these tasks has not been identified.

The main portfolio capacity risk is allowing a broad national catalogue to become an unbounded obligation before the need and stewardship model are understood.

## Strategic Fit and Duplication

The opportunity appears compatible with Fibonacci OS, but it may duplicate existing public, community, regional, or commercial directories. External landscape research is required before treating the creation of a new directory as the best portfolio action.

There may be greater leverage in improving an existing resource, linking resources, or publishing a reusable structure and maintenance practice rather than owning a complete catalogue.

## Opportunity Cost and Concentration Risk

Attention spent on a public directory may displace work on the operating system's artefacts, workflows, or a reference venture. A directory also concentrates reputational and ethical risk in the accuracy of maintained information.

The opportunity cost is currently unquantified because the active portfolio and available capacity are unknown.

## Value, Learning, and Sustainability Considerations

The project could produce public value and teach Fibonacci OS about evidence stewardship, accessibility, human escalation, and maintenance. Its value would not be measured only by traffic or revenue.

Its sustainability depends on a bounded scope, clear ownership, reviewable provenance, and a credible way to stop or transfer maintenance if the project no longer deserves attention.

## Options and Consequences

### Option A: Defer

Conduct no project work until the active portfolio, existing resources, and available capacity are understood. This reduces immediate risk but delays learning.

### Option B: Run a bounded discovery experiment

Test a few tasks and entries without committing to a national directory. This creates useful evidence while limiting scope, but still consumes research and review capacity.

### Option C: Begin a national directory build

This could create a visible asset quickly but would accept substantial unknown maintenance, governance, duplication, and safety risk. Current evidence does not support this option.

## Recommendation

If capacity exists, choose Option B. Treat it as an experiment with an explicit stop condition and portfolio review. Otherwise choose Option A until capacity and duplication are understood. Do not choose Option C on current evidence.

## Confidence

Medium that a bounded experiment is more portfolio-compatible than a national build; low because the actual portfolio and capacity data are missing.

## Requested Human Decision

Confirm whether this opportunity deserves a bounded experiment within current capacity, and identify which existing work would be delayed or protected if it proceeds.
